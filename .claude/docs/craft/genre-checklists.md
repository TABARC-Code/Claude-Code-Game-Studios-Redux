# Genre Checklists

Read by `/design-craft` (Phase 1), `/exploit-hunt` (Phase 3), `/table-feel`, `/tempo-curve` and
`/mechanic-forge`.

One short block per genre. **Checks** are questions the design should answer in its rules.
**Risks** are failures to probe in playtest. Hybrids take the blocks of each parent. Every item is
a rule of thumb, not a requirement; a design may break one deliberately if it says why.

## Worker placement
- **Checks:** is a taken space blocked, bumpable, or pay-to-share? Can a space be reserved ahead?
  How do workers grow, and what does each extra worker cost? Does turn order rotate, and is first
  player worth a space?
- **Risks:** one must-take space every round; late seats starved; a worker count that snowballs;
  idle players with nothing to read during long turns.

## Deckbuilder
- **Checks:** market type (fixed piles, rotating row, personal offer); a way to thin the deck and
  its cost; end trigger tied to the market; how much a player sees before buying.
- **Risks:** an infinite or near-infinite combo with no ceiling; one card bought every game;
  thinning so cheap the deck becomes one card; parallel play with no reason to watch others.

## Engine builder
- **Checks:** when the engine starts paying back (the crossover turn); a reason to stop building
  and convert; something shared that engines compete over.
- **Risks:** a late game that is all payout with no decisions; first-built engine always winning;
  multiplayer solitaire; scoring collapse once one engine part is denied.

## Area control
- **Checks:** how presence is measured (majority, threshold, ranked); when areas score (each round,
  end, triggered); how a leader is reached and pushed back; ties.
- **Risks:** turtling; pile-on against the leader becoming the only strategy (toxic optimum);
  kingmaking by trailing players; first scoring round deciding the game.

## Co-op
- **Checks:** crisis cadence (how often things get worse, and how visibly); recovery windows; hidden
  or limited information that stops one player running the table; a clear loss state.
- **Risks:** quarterbacking; a difficulty that is solved after a few plays; one bad draw that loses
  the game with no response; a lull where nothing threatens.

## Hidden role
- **Checks:** role mix by player count; actions beyond talk that reveal or test roles; what an
  eliminated or exposed player does next; a teach line on lying being expected.
- **Risks:** the first accusation deciding the game; quiet players ignored; elimination leaving
  players idle; discomfort at mixed tables where deception is not welcome.

## Legacy and campaign
- **Checks:** types of permanent change (rules added, pieces destroyed, board altered); catch-up for
  a player who missed or lost sessions; a reset or replay option; the end state of the campaign.
- **Risks:** a runaway player after early wins; a broken state from a permanent change nobody
  tested; dead sessions late in the campaign; stacked bonuses with no ceiling.

## Roll-and-write
- **Checks:** input shape (shared roll, private roll, drafted dice); what the sheet constrains;
  how a bad roll is mitigated; solo scaling.
- **Risks:** one optimal fill order; no reason to look at other sheets; long scoring at the end;
  a sheet too dense to read.

## Party
- **Checks:** teach under about two minutes; play from about four to past six without rework;
  spectator value between turns; no reading or maths gate for the core action.
- **Risks:** one player dominating the talk; downtime that grows with count; humour that relies on
  one group's norms; elimination at a social game.

## Puzzle
- **Checks:** constraint density (enough to reason, not so much it is arithmetic); a hint or
  undo path; difficulty ramp across puzzles; a reason brute force fails.
- **Risks:** a solution found by trying everything; one stuck point ending the session; puzzles
  that are hard only because rules are unclear; co-op puzzles solved by the strongest solver alone.

## Dexterity
- **Checks:** reset speed between turns; table footprint; variants for different hands and
  reach (accessibility); a scoring rule that does not need disputes.
- **Risks:** one physical trick dominating; long resets killing pace; arguments over a near miss;
  players excluded by grip or reach.

## Racing
- **Checks:** catch-up that is not rubber-banding (drafting, slipstream, cheaper moves when behind);
  hazards or blocking on the track; a visible finish.
- **Risks:** the leader decided early; catch-up so strong that leading is pointless; no
  interaction beyond position; the last lap being arithmetic.

## 4X and civilisation
- **Checks:** upkeep or sinks that stop endless expansion; tech or upgrade timing windows; conflict
  that is decisive without ending a player's game; a session length the map can actually deliver.
- **Risks:** an early land grab deciding everything; one player eliminated and idle for hours;
  overstay after the winner is clear; bookkeeping that slows late turns.

## Economic and share
- **Checks:** liquidity (how cash cycles and where it runs short); obsolescence as tempo control;
  how share price moves and who controls it; a readable net worth during play.
- **Risks:** one player cornering a company; a dead early game before money moves; maths load at
  the end; kingmaking through share dumps.

## Skirmish
- **Checks:** activation model and first-strike risk; the lethality arc across rounds; scenario
  objectives that pull pieces into contact; forces of a size the table and time allow.
- **Risks:** first round deciding it; linear grind with no tipping point; one unit stronger than a
  force; small-format swing (see `negative-feedback.md`).

## Mass battle
- **Checks:** command and control (what the commander can and cannot direct); morale and rout with
  rally rules; supply or reserves; a turn length that survives the piece count.
- **Risks:** downtime for the non-active player; a line that cannot be broken; bookkeeping per
  piece; an end condition that never fires before time runs out.

## Card duel
- **Checks:** resource curve (how costs ramp); card advantage and how it is gained; an answer for
  every threat category; mulligan or draw smoothing.
- **Risks:** a first-turn win or lock; one deck dominating the field; non-interactive turns;
  feel-bad draws that decide the game before play starts.

## Solo
- **Checks:** what the opponent or system is (bot, deck, puzzle, score target); how the bot decides,
  in steps short enough to run without errors; difficulty levels; a reason to replay.
- **Risks:** bot upkeep heavier than the player's turn; a score target that is trivial after a few
  plays; no surprise once the deck is known; rules that assume a second player's judgement.
