# Card families

Read by `/classic-borrow` (Phase 3) and `/card-forge`.

Traditional card play falls into a handful of families. Each one generates its own tension, win type and
information state, and each has at least one mechanism a modern design can lift.

| Family | Core tension | Win type | Information | Mechanism worth borrowing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trick-taking | when to spend a strong card | most tricks or points from tricks | hidden hands, revealed play | follow-suit obligation; trumps; bidding your own target |
| Rummy and melding | hold for a better set, or lay down now | first out, or points from melds | hidden hand, public melds and discards | taking the discard reveals intent; penalty for cards still held |
| Shedding | get rid of cards, block others | first to empty | hidden hand, public pile | matching limits; special cards that skip or reverse |
| Climbing | beat the last play or pass | first out, with ranks carrying to the next hand | hidden hand | passing locks you out of the current round; bombs beat any shape |
| Patience | constraint chains against a draw clock | clearing a layout | partly hidden layout | ruled columns; limited free cells as a resource |
| Trading and market | price and timing of exchange | most value or set collection | open market, hidden holdings | a market row that slides and gets cheaper with age |
| Betting | commit before the reveal | chips | hidden hands, public bets | hand hierarchy with escalating stakes; folding as a real option |
| Matching | memory and pattern spotting | most pairs or sets | face-down grid | information that becomes public when it is used |
| Speed | reaction and recognition | first to act correctly | fully open | simultaneous play with no turns |

Notes:

- Running a deck solo, as a patience game, is a quick check of its internal logic before anyone else
  plays it.
- Borrowing a whole family model (say, trick-taking as a combat system) is legitimate. Check the tension
  still fits your core and record the lineage.
- Rule details for many traditional games vary by region and era. Describe the mechanism, not a
  definitive historical rule.
